The Mahā-Mantra — your daily anchor.

Chanting the holy names is the simplest, most direct practice in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Anyone, anywhere, can begin today.

The Mahā-Mantra
Hare Kṛṣṇa · Hare Kṛṣṇa · Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa · Hare Hare
Hare Rāma · Hare Rāma · Rāma Rāma · Hare Hare

"O energy of the Lord, O Lord, please engage me in Your service." Sixteen names, sixteen openings of the heart. Chanted aloud, softly, or in the mind — all are pleasing to Kṛṣṇa.

1

Find a tulasi mālā

A mālā has 108 wooden beads plus a sumeru (head bead). You can collect one at any ISKCON temple. Until then, count on your fingers.

Don't have one yet? Use the counter below to begin today.
2

Sit, breathe, settle

Choose a clean, quiet spot. Sit comfortably with the spine upright. Take three slow breaths. Bring the mind to Kṛṣṇa.

Early morning (4–7 AM) is the most potent time — but any time is better than none.
3

Chant one bead at a time

Hold the mālā in your right hand. Begin at the bead next to the sumeru. Chant the full mantra on each bead, then move to the next.

108 beads = one round. Never cross the sumeru — reverse direction instead.
4

Build the habit

Start with one round a day. Soon you'll want two, then four, then sixteen — the traditional commitment of an initiated devotee.

Consistency outweighs quantity. One round daily, every day, for life.
