Home · How to be Happy

The science behind lasting joy.

A six-part presentation drawing on the Bhagavad-gītā, observed psychology, and 5,000 years of contemplative testing. Use the arrows, dots, or arrow keys to navigate.

Slide 1 / 6

The problem is universal.

Every human being — across every culture, every century — seeks one thing: happiness without interruption. Yet despite unprecedented wealth, comfort, and entertainment, anxiety and dissatisfaction are at all-time highs. Why?

Slide 2 / 6

Material happiness has a ceiling.

The Bhagavad-gītā observes that whatever happiness comes from the senses has a beginning and an end. Like a pleasant breeze — refreshing, but never lasting. Each pleasure dulls; we chase the next.

"That happiness which is derived from contact of the senses with their objects appears like nectar at first, but is poison in the end." Bhagavad-gītā 18.38
Slide 3 / 6

You are not the body.

The Gītā opens with a radical premise: the conscious self — the ātmā — is not the body. The body is a vehicle; you are the driver. All confusion about happiness begins when we mistake the vehicle for the self.

"The soul is neither born, nor does it ever die; nor having once existed, does it ever cease to be." Bhagavad-gītā 2.20
Slide 4 / 6

The four unavoidable miseries.

Regardless of how we arrange our lives, four conditions await every embodied being: birth, disease, old age, and death. The intelligent ask: is there a happiness beyond these? The answer of the Vedic sages is yes — and it is the only happiness worth pursuing.

Slide 5 / 6

The formula: serve, chant, surrender.

Lasting joy is not built — it is uncovered. It is the natural state of the soul in connection with its source. Three time-tested practices restore that connection: service to the divine and to devotees, chanting of the holy names, and surrender of the false ego.

Slide 6 / 6

Try the experiment.

Don't take this on faith. The Bhāgavata tradition is a science — set up the lab, run the experiment, observe the results. Chant the mahā-mantra daily for forty days. Eat sanctified food. Spend time in the temple. Watch what shifts.

"Always think of Me, become My devotee, worship Me and offer your homage unto Me. Thus you will come to Me without fail." Bhagavad-gītā 9.34
